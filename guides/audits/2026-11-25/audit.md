# Where Adaptive3 still needs the human

**15 of 36 builds entered exceptional Backstop (42%). The goal is at most 2%.** Thirteen builds presented at least one Backstop question. The same pattern appears on the latest recipe revision, where 13 of 31 builds reached Backstop. The November 23 audit found 36 of 90 (40%). These cohorts differ in workload and completion, so the percentages establish the size of the gap rather than a trend. [Design goals](../../adaptive3/PRINCIPLES.md#iteration-and-recovery) · [November 23 audit](../2026-11-23/audit.md) · [Job inventory](inventory.md)

At the cutoff, eight builds had succeeded, two were cancelled, 15 were waiting for a human, ten were waiting for child jobs and one was active. Across the 36 builds there were 86 review messages and 69 human replies. Twenty builds presented more than one message. Of the 30 messages following an earlier one in the same review thread, 14 carried exactly the same attachments. **All 14 followed a written reply.** Sometimes that was precisely what the human wanted. In the Pulse Docker job, the person asked whether verification actually used Docker. The agent cited the real daemon and six passing Linux suites, kept the same packet, received approval and merged. [Pulse Docker review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaPwz3Elhwj2eKo4UnOvotOfeg)

The clearest source of confusion was an outcome review that told the human **“Your acceptance is recorded; no new decision is needed.”** The job was waiting on **Accept and merge**. The person had chosen **Review before Complete** on the plan, so the stop was correct. The message treated verification as final approval. Generate the human question from the decision the recipe is actually waiting for, using the author’s summary to explain what changed. ## Where the human had to intervene

**1. The delegate can choose the right action and still send it to the human.** In the macVZ job, the human explained that a node could request a Mac base in the same job and request the tools it needed. The next author call hit model capacity. The delegate chose one local retry and explicitly marked the instruction as preserving scope. The recorded state showed that a retry was available, but the action was ineligible. The recipe requires a human receipt on the current delegate turn whenever the agent fills in the instruction field. That turn had none. A 695-word review went to the human, who replied, “Try again. Model capacity is typically a transient issue.” In a separate Root job, the delegate retried two capacity failures before asking about the third. Keep the bounded retry and give a local repair a route that can carry its instructions. [macVZ job](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2kRI3WNr7CDRbWhR3EXS65U) · [Eligibility rule](../../../recipes/adaptive3/backstop-action.yaml#L13) · [Root retry sequence](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaNDEVLudVOluo964MsstJ1UcK)

**2. Agents ask the human to do coordination they can do themselves.** The requester directed c2j to store node status directly in the chapter. JobDB confirmed that its pinned version lacked the field. The author, reviewer and delegate converged on asking whether c2j could *open a negotiation*. No offer had been made and accepting a change was still a separate decision. In the Cortex project switcher, a real 403 led to requests for Lab operator contacts, then a new authorization design. The human had to restate the code-only scope, exclude new authentication and point the author to embedded JobDB. The job presented eight messages, five in Backstop. Let the executing cell open a negotiation for a requested outcome and test at the boundary it owns. Bring back an actual tradeoff, such as an incompatible offer or a credential grant. [Node status](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MbmPf0wjAWwY0E8QulCUrBn1ds) · [Project switcher](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaLY2L6g3nUBQneygaCbyE2GKa) · [Example of a real credential decision](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2idjFZQDjwPpgmu1X6NRzIj)

**3. One parent decision becomes several human conversations.** Root was explicitly asked to open jobs for missing verification. Its 17 direct Adaptive3 children produced 32 review messages, and eight children were waiting on the human at cutoff. The person explained the retirement of Intel macOS in the c2j, Cortex and JobDB jobs. The first wording in two threads was ambiguous enough to justify clarifying once. JobDB later received the explicit instruction to remove the platform. Its delegate said the instruction settled the scope, then requested another plan approval. A parent should record a shared scope decision once and pass it to affected children. An individual child should bring back a conflicting cell requirement or a genuine local exception. This family accounts for six exceptional builds. Another nine of the 19 builds outside that group also escalated. [Root assignment](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaOPBDDZLCS4oOjSTRxo6IeDHY) · [c2j](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2gTny122DL2EiQOER4KjSvT) · [Cortex](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2ipKxKyq1tLaSaXbVyiudCz) · [JobDB](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2ed5BEnYN9cy6OZRVzsRuuE)

**4. Presentation and closure become decisions.** Infra’s reviewer correctly spotted an unclear attachment label. The recipe itself replaces the supplied metadata for negotiation files. The author added clear links, the reviewer passed them, and the human still received a question about whether Preparation could proceed with those links. In the VZ macOS job the human withdrew the assignment while cancelling a child review. The source-job delegate accurately reported that no implementation decision remained, then opened another review. Cancellation of a child alone does not settle its parent. Here the person also explicitly withdrew the work. Have the parent record that disposition and keep mechanical presentation fixes with the component that renders them. [Infra review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2eb7O7VVwK0McOyRKHzHwB6) · [Attachment metadata](../../../recipes/adaptive3/tools.yaml#L109) · [Withdrawal](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2haaC0PgRUrbmwZFl2cvRyV)

**5. The author writes as if the human saw an internal draft.** In four of the 31 builds that presented a review, the very first message or its primary document explicitly said “since the last review” or “since the previous review.” All four had an author revise work after an internal reviewer’s finding. None had called the job’s delegate before that form. The problem can also cross stages. A JobDB outcome told the person “Since your last review, I updated the report only.” Their last decision had been to approve the plan. Implementation, test repairs and successful verification all followed that decision. Only the *internal reviewer’s* last request was about the report. [Four first reviews and the JobDB sequence](inventory.md#which-earlier-review-could-the-person-have-seen)

The author prompt already asks for changes since the version the human saw. The author receives the current findings and any human reply. The review thread and its earlier documents are available downstream, after the author has written the message and the 1P. The recipe can then send that summary directly to the person. Give the writer the last review actually presented for this decision, when one exists. The existing review thread can supply that reference. ## What to keep

| Design goal | What the jobs show |
|---|---|
| Few sessions with distinct roles | All 36 builds used one primary planning-author session apiece. All 21 reaching Implementation used one primary work-author session apiece. All 35 with an independent main assessment used one main reviewer session apiece. Owner consultations and the delegate used additional sessions. |
| A short path for small changes | Eight builds entered Implementation before Preparation. Four later reached Preparation. The mobile-title job exposed a real truncation bug in live pending messages. Nine unrelated browser failures also reproduced on the base. The first belonged with the implementer, the second with the failing suite’s owner. [Mobile-title job](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MbmW2BxcHJdtm2umSa6N0NlWzM) |
| A recognizable, readable review | All 42 Preparation messages presented the 1P first with a `1P:` document title, Design and Tests. The message itself was often costly to read. The median was 111.5 words across all 86 messages, and 57 were at least 100 words long. Put the decision and what changed in the first two sentences. Keep evidence in the attachments. |
| Bounded consultation and useful review | Twelve builds entered 30 consultation workflows. The maximum observed in one phase visit was four, below the ten-thread allowance. Preserve the four-round per-conversation limit. The pgjobdb reviewer correctly distinguished a fourth failing check that the earlier exception expressly excluded. [Release-gate decision](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3Ma82aw2kRdhI9UvgfMU7AbWS8Z) |

The 26 Backstop visits came from Preparation 13 times, Direction ten times and Implementation three times. On its first attempt in those 26 visits, the delegate chose to ask 19 times and to resume six times. One delegate call failed. Ten visits immediately followed a failed agent operation. Separately, the delegate screened six repeat reviews, continued under existing approval once and asked five times. Direction accumulated 109 cycles across 60 visits, and Preparation accumulated 110 across 54 visits. Fifteen builds revisited Direction and 13 revisited Preparation. I would keep the author/reviewer split and fix the decision routes first. [Stage sequences for every build](inventory.md)

## Correction order

1. **Fix the local-retry route.** Replay the macVZ case with the earlier receipt, unchanged scope and available retry. It should return to its author without a form. Keep a counterexample where the requested repair would actually change scope.
2. **Give the coordinator shared decisions and withdrawals.** Replay one retired-platform decision across sibling jobs, and the explicit VZ withdrawal from a child review. Verify that a genuine child exception still reaches its owner.
3. **Write from the person’s place in the job.** On the first plan, present the proposal and the decision, even after several internal edits. On a repeat, explain the material difference from the last packet presented in that thread. If the person asked a question and the proposal stands, answer it directly. On the first outcome after plan approval, summarize what was delivered and verified against that plan, then ask for the decision still open. Apply this to both the message and the primary document. Replay the Cortex first plan and its genuine revision, the JobDB outcome after a report edit, and the parallel-node merge question. Preserve the same-thread Docker answer as a healthy case.
4. **Keep routine work off the human’s desk.** Exercise three cases together: opening a needed negotiation, repairing recipe-generated attachment labels and routing a base failure to its owner while fixing a local defect. Measure exceptional builds, human forms per parent request and repeated decisions on the next cohort. Count requested final reviews and clarification answers separately.
