# What people asked for, and why they got asked for an operator

The [consolidated improvement plan](improvement-plan.md) includes the changes proposed here and in the review audit.

**32 of 36 Adaptive3 jobs asked for a change. Eleven of 86 review messages asked someone to identify an owner, connect the job to an operator, or confirm access or readiness on an operator’s behalf.** Seven jobs generated those eleven messages. Four children of Root’s verify-hook audit generated eight of them. The clearest failure was asking a person to establish in advance what the job’s own verify run could establish. A later request for help from the very same job was warranted by an actual failed run. [Review-by-review evidence](inventory.md#requests-for-an-owner-or-operator)

## Where the work went sideways

**Planning turned “we haven’t run it” into “find someone who can let us run it.”** The c2j Backstop message acknowledged that the hook could submit children, then asked for a provisioning operator or a receipt for two routes. No candidate tests had run. The person explained that the hook runs before merge and its operations can be inspected. The author removed the prerequisite. Later, its Apple Silicon verification child recorded six leases and no test result. At that point Pulse had a specific run to diagnose. The record establishes that the child produced no result. It leaves the cause open. Similarly, macVZ asked twice for a Mac operator or toolchain confirmation, even after being reminded that a node can request a different base in the same job. [c2j review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2gTny122DL2EiQOER4KjSvT) · [Apple Silicon child](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MbjBfYF4QgmFoxvmGc4SUEynuN) · [macVZ review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3MaP2kRI3WNr7CDRbWhR3EXS65U)

**Application debugging became a search for deployment owners.** Cortex consulted pgjobdb, Infra and Root, then asked the person for Lab contacts. The 403 was real. The new Cortex endpoint rejected the browser request before it reached JobDB. After the person pointed to embedded JobDB, the agent demonstrated list, switch, create and reopen against that setup. A separate browser-suite failure still needed work. Of 30 formal consultation visits across the cohort, only **two** went directly to Infra, from this job and from c2j looking for a tenant provisioning contact. Cortex switching, Cortex verification, c2j verification and macVZ together accounted for 15 of the 30. Some of those visits investigated actual failures. **Publication language is common. Its meaning varies.** Thirty-six of 86 messages across 18 jobs used a form of *publish*, *publication* or *deploy*. That includes statements such as “No deployment was performed” on a first design review. All seven successful change jobs had a successful merge receipt and a skipped publication result because the cell had no publish hook configured. The eighth successful job was the capacity explanation. The JobDB timestamp assignment also explicitly required a fresh Go consumer to fetch the merged commit and exercise it. Its outcome marked that check pending. The producer and queue histories establish the merge and skipped hook. I found no post-merge consumer run in those two histories. The pgjobdb adopter later used the matching pin. That establishes adoption, while the producer’s requested consumer test remains unverified here. A publication question did uncover a real decision. The person asked JobDB to stop push-triggered tests. That workflow was also the only recorded caller for automatic tagging and release. The person chose to disable it and defer the replacement publisher. In pgjobdb a new concurrency failure fell outside an earlier exception, so retaining the check and seeking an upstream fix made sense. The subsequent request for a maintainer contact confused who should carry the accepted work forward. ## What the human actually needed

The recipe presented 42 Preparation messages, 28 Backstop messages, 15 Outcome messages and one Direction answer. People responded 69 times. Twenty-four approved without text. I read the other 45 and assigned each its main purpose. A mixed reply counts once. Scope includes both changing a requirement and restating one the agent had missed.

| Main purpose of written reply | Count | Example |
|---|---:|---|
| Set, narrow or restate the desired behavior | 21 | Drop Intel Mac, keep Apple Silicon, put status directly in the chapter |
| Ask for an explanation, evidence or an investigation | 11 | Which metadata APIs exist? Does the Docker verification actually use Docker? |
| Correct how or where the job should do its work | 7 | Run against embedded JobDB, request packages, inspect the job’s own verification |
| Resume after an execution or capacity failure | 3 | Retry the interrupted author turn |
| Select an offered option or say to proceed | 3 | Choose the seven-op metrics rollout |

**Repeated pushback sometimes improved the decision.** On labels, the person remembered a JobDB metadata feature. Root checked the pinned API and 14 recent commits. A worker could update a payload during leased work, but a client had no standalone edit on a finished job. The person recognized the earlier work had never landed and asked Root to recover it. On parallel jobs the correction went both ways. The person was right that c2j could use existing JobDB waits for first failure. The agent distinguished detecting failure from cancelling siblings, for which it had not verified the available access. The person chose to ship `wait_all` first. Both exchanges reward a precise explanation, even when it takes more than one reply. ## What I’d change in the prompts

1. **Give the planning author and reviewer the same short description of the job.** “You’re working in this cell’s job. For a change, the implementer makes the change and runs focused checks. The recipe runs the cell’s verify hook on the candidate before merge and returns failures for repair. A hook step can request its base, capabilities and packages. Inspect the run when you need to know what happened.” Today the cell and job ID are present on every agent turn, but the clearest lifecycle explanation arrives in the implementation instructions. Several jobs got stuck earlier. [Session context](../../../recipes/adaptive3/prompts/context.yaml#L5) · [Implementation instructions](../../../recipes/adaptive3/prompts/execute.yaml#L81) · [Hook contract](../../BUILD_HOOKS_USER_GUIDE.md#start-with-verification)
2. **Make the delegate narrow a limitation to the evidence.** Give the attempted operation, the result, and what remains possible in the cell. “This candidate’s Mac child recorded six leases and produced no test output” gives Pulse something to investigate. “Mac access is unconfirmed” is a reason to try the ordinary declared route. A documented access restriction or an actual grant decision can justify going straight to its owner. When the job needs an external fix, the agent can open the negotiation itself. Bring the person the unresolved choice.
3. **Make completion specific to the request.** For an explanation, answer it. For a change, carry it through verification, the requested review and merge. When a publish hook applies, follow it to a successful result or report its specific failure. If the requester needs proof from the released artifact, arrange that check with the producer or coordinator and report its result. If the *publishing mechanism* needs to change, the coordinator sequences a separate contribution with the owning cell. Keep operator contacts and deployment status out of ordinary review text unless they change the person’s decision. The agent guide already describes a narrow human question for a required non-Git dependency missing after the producer has finished. Keep those instructions at that point of need. [Working in C2](../../WORKING_IN_C2.md#dependencies-and-publication) · [Merge queue publication](../../../recipes/adaptive3/merge-queue-batch.yaml#L65) · [Publisher change boundary](../../../recipes/adaptive3/publisher-scope.yaml)

For the next cohort, count owner requests before a candidate run separately from those after a recorded failure or a documented grant requirement. Keep the Root family separate. Recheck whether explicitly requested post-merge checks have results, and whether evidence-backed answers still resolve disputed assumptions. A lower message count alone would miss both outcomes.
