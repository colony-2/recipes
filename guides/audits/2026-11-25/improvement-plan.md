## Delegate orchestration

Give the delegate ownership of the next action and the handoff between primitives. Replace the earlier proposal to screen pending human questions with a single outward path. The delegate sees every proposed message, document or interview package before it reaches the requester. The top-level recipe records one dispatch, runs the primitive and returns its result to the delegate. Keep the concrete checks and waits inside the operations that own them. ## Follow-up — let a job finish when its assignment is complete

Let either author say “this job has done what was asked” and point to the result. The delegate can reach the same conclusion during recovery. **What, if anything, does this job still owe the requester?** Use the existing review, evidence and approvals to answer. If the assignment is fulfilled, return the result and finish. A waiting parent receives it like any other caller. Otherwise, resume at the first unfinished action this job owns. Bring the person back for a requested final review or a decision only they can make.

Today an accepted Outcome with a changed worktree goes to integration. The Cortex child shows where that rule breaks down. Its parent owned combined verification and merge. The child had saved the requested patch and evidence in task 47, and its focused test passed twice. Its last message said the person had nothing to do, yet its only button was **Revise**. [Outcome routing](../../../adaptive3.yaml#L354) · [Child job](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3McL7OKBZqemgtqqAoNVkBu4agp) · [Parent assignment](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/UiOXfJCyb3nLw5G7TmWyq0Z7jOs)

Test the same change under two assignments. “Give me the result so I can integrate it” should finish once the requester can use it. “Land this change” should continue through merge. A missing promised check should send either job back for that check. The assignment determines which action remains.

## Follow-up — put questions beside the passage they concern

Ask the review client to write questions inline in the 1P, Design, Tests or other review documents using CriticMarkup. For example, `Existing jobs keep their settings. {>>Does this include jobs already waiting for review?<<}` A person can type a plain comment and let the client supply the markup. Preserve who asked each question, including questions written by an agent.

Use the existing annotated-document attachment in the same review thread. The author answers a clarification against the passage as written. If the requester asks for a change, the author revises the document and says what changed. A general question can still go in the review message, and comments stay optional on approval. Test a question in the 1P and a requested edit in Tests in the same reply. The author should answer the first and revise only the passage identified by the second. ## Follow-up — match the review to the message

**Recipes.** Let an agent answer a comment in the thread. Keep the original plan and any pending approval accessible there. A clarification can end with the answer. When the person gives direction, record what it settles and continue. If the remaining work belongs to an owner, send it there. Open a decision for the requester when their answer would change what happens or when a promised approval is due. [November 26 audit](../2026-11-26/audit.md)

**Documents in the existing review flow.** Put the 1P first for the first plan approval, with Design and Tests attached. When asking someone to review a document edit, open that document and say what changed since they saw it. For a question or answer, put enough context in the message to read it on its own. Quote a passage if the wording matters. Send that turn through `review/v2` with `documents: {}` and omit `overview_document_id`. The earlier documents and their approval stay available in the same thread. [Review contract](../../../recipes/review-v2.py#L182)

**Cortex.** In the thread, show the message and its reply control first. For a document review, open the selected primary. For a message with optional evidence, show named links and let the reader open them. Honor that choice on the current turn as well as in history. Omitting the overview alone still opens the first attachment in the current reader. Recipes and Cortex should agree how a message distinguishes optional attachments from a primary to read. [Current reader](https://github.com/colony-2/cortex/blob/0c4aebcfb7d1b48363581c710a1b2b9777b1de86/web/app/src/components/ReviewV2.tsx#L61)

**Words and controls.** Use **Reply** for a question, an observation or a requested edit. Let the receiving agent interpret it. On an actual decision, label the action with what it does. Keep the review-before-completion preference the person already chose. A material new decision can ask, for example, whether to include a visible layout change. Its answer should settle that choice without another request to approve the supporting checks.

If the delegate and the author disagree about what a person approved, read the actual question and reply together. Correct the account shown to the person before asking for anything. In the scroll case, the prompt and 1P must agree on whether the eight-pixel change is still a proposal. Compare the related repair before opening another decision.

### Start with what the requester knows

The author and delegate can assume the person knows what they asked for, what they decided, and what someone explained to them in this thread. An internal review, a delegate’s decision, or a term buried in an attachment needs an introduction. When earlier context matters, remind the reader in one sentence. Explain a new concept by saying what it does and why it matters here. Leave out names, versions and test commands unless the person needs them to decide.

For a repeat approval, say what the person last agreed to, what has changed for them, and what their answer would settle. Recommend a course when there is a choice. If the next step already follows from their instructions, take it. An author can upgrade a dependency to deliver the agreed change and can arrange a separate prerequisite job when the upgrade is worth testing and landing on its own.

- **Lease timestamps.** “Cell archiving shipped while we were adding lease timestamps. I’ll bring the timestamp change up to date and check that upgrading an existing database preserves its archived cells.” The version numbers belong in the engineering record. If the upgrade would leave some users unable to open their data, establish who is affected and bring that specific choice to the requester. [Timestamp review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/3Ma82aw2kRdhI9UvgfMU7AbWS8Z)
- **Interrupted jobs.** “You asked for work to resume after a connection failure. We found that an older worker could pick up a job it wouldn’t know how to resume. We’ll check that the worker supports retries before it starts the job. That worked in a small test. We still need to try the full workflow.” If this is the first plan approval, close with “Shall we implement this?” If the person already approved that behavior, the author can carry on. [Timed-retry review](https://jobs.colony2.com/c2/jobs/Cu60BU3yaA1xSW9r0xLeqyYydQ2)

Replay the labels clarification, the timed-retry explanation and the DAG instruction to combine fixes. Pair a compatible database upgrade with a case where an upgrade would drop support for existing data. The first should advance under the existing direction. The second should explain whose data is affected and ask what to do. Preserve first plan approvals and requested final reviews in both cases.

Read each rendered message with the attachments closed. Can someone tell what changed for them and, if a reply is needed, what it will decide? Check the action the job actually takes. ## Planning interviews with Refine

**Ask directly** when an agent already knows the question and can say what the answer would settle. Give the person enough context to answer. **Use a prepared interview** when the job needs to find out whether a whole proposal matches what the person expects. A proposal with no open questions can still solve the wrong problem. The Refine UI could serve both kinds of engagement.

1. **The planner writes a proposal.** The 1P explains the problem as the planner understands it, what someone would experience if the proposal worked, and what falls outside it. The 1P, Design and Tests are private source material for agents. Include the reasoning, examples, alternatives and assumptions that explain how the planner got there. Suggested questions and paths are useful inputs.
2. **Refine examines the whole proposal.** Look for choices implicit in the framing, design and tests, including choices the planner treats as settled. Prepare plain-language questions about the assumptions that matter most if wrong. Generate likely follow-ups in advance so the person can cover several choices in one sitting. Let them challenge the premise and skip branches that no longer apply. End when the person stops or the remaining questions have little value.
3. **Every interview returns to the planner as actual Q&A.** Most responses are multiple choice. Always offer **Other** with room to type. For each question shown, return its exact wording, every choice in display order, the selection, and any typed words verbatim. Preserve skips and corrections. Speculative branches stay in the generation material.
4. **The planner judges how well the plan fits.** Update the parts the answers change. When the plan appears aligned with what the person wants, hand it to implementation under the job’s existing direction. Say what to deliver, which constraints come from the person, which assumptions remain, and what the implementer can decide. Link the plan and actual answers. Investigate technical uncertainties and request another round only for consequential choices still open. If permission to act is separately due, ask about the specific action.

Keep a transcript of rounds. Each planner turn points to its source documents and says what is new. The interview record itself is Refine’s entire reply. A fresh planner can change the focus and still use that history. When preparing another batch, carry earlier answers forward. Reopen a settled choice only when something changes its consequences or reveals a conflict. Tell the person why it matters now.

**Replay a plan that solves the wrong problem.** A requester asks for easier reviews. The 1P proposes shorter documents and lists no open questions. Refine asks, “If every review took thirty seconds to read, but you were asked just as often, would that solve it?” The person says they want fewer interruptions. The planner should rethink when to involve them. The remaining questions about document length can be dropped.

**Replay a missing choice.** Refine asks “When should we notify you about a job that keeps retrying?” and offers “Immediately,” “After ten minutes” and “Other.” The person picks ten minutes. Seeing the menu lets the planner notice a missing option, “Only when I need to act.” Include it in the next batch if it would change the work and earlier guidance leaves the choice open. Also replay a typed **Other** answer and an early stop. Check that the planner receives the exact text and can distinguish skipped questions from unshown ones. In the next audit, count return visits for questions we could have anticipated in the first sitting.
