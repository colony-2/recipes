# Why Adaptive3 jobs keep coming back for another decision

**Backstop has become a routine way to resume work the implementer already owns.** Across the latest 200 recipe jobs, 90 were Adaptive3 builds. Thirty-six of those builds reached Backstop. The most common trip was Implementation → Backstop → Implementation, 38 times across 17 builds. The detailed audit includes the decision sequence for every job and the investigator’s first answer at all 79 Backstop visits. The recommendations describe how to shorten that path within Adaptive3. [Detailed audit](inventory.md) · [Recommendations](improvement-plan.md)

### 1. A real defect and an unnecessary escalation can happen together

The Cortex inbox job is the clearest example. Its implementer was getting through a browser suite. Different tests failed as earlier ones were fixed. On one return, the recovery agent identified two stale Refresh locators and the existing menu action needed to fix them. Backstop then sent the saved candidate to the same implementer. The history contains eleven such trips, five begun on the replacement job itself. Across the cohort the investigator’s **first** answer was “resume” on 49 of 79 visits, “ask” on 24, and unavailable during Git failures on six. “Resume” alone does not establish that all 49 could have been handled automatically. The same-stage trips with a concrete repair are the best starting point. [Cortex inbox](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUIvB1f07hNHDcDGVHCpokBCtb)

The infrastructure issue was real. Seven jobs ended with the same unreachable Git mirror, including six builds whose Backstop form could not start. Excluding those six leaves **30/84 builds** with Backstop. Excluding builds with inherited tasks as well leaves **21/72**. Both rates are far from the desired one or two jobs per hundred. The 200 histories cover multiple deployed revisions. None used the latest checkout’s counter-reset fix, so we cannot judge its production effect yet. [Rates and revision breakdown](inventory.md#which-rate-to-use)

### 2. Agents can agree on the wrong assignment

A Cortex feature needed to create a job that waited on another job. A receiver-recovery problem led to a JobDB child with an assignment to reserve a goal for an authenticated bridge. Its one-pager identified the parent as the requester. Two rounds of assessment in Direction and two in Preparation made the reservation design more detailed. The cancellation explained that the original request was simply to add a wait on a job ID. The child had the source of the request in writing. What it lacked was a reason to question whether the **parent’s proposed mechanism** belonged in the human’s request. [Parent](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUImjkpDzNtSuO4xuzYr9ijYxo) · [Child](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/FSfzVHCD63fKGZDNpNzKNebE57G)

Native receipts already distinguish human replies. All 219 collected review responses found under the build phases carried a human actor in the receipt preview. That says who clicked or wrote the response. It does not turn every claim inside a reviewed document, a delegated prompt or an agent’s paraphrase into an independent human instruction. In a separate project-switcher job, the reviewer had to explain that there was no separate catalog or credential file, then clarify which backend was actually in use. These are observed scope corrections. The logs cannot tell us whether a model privately believed a human had authored the earlier proposals. [Project-switcher job](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUJ9HshLaK9WMP7VficJq1NUD0) · [Current review routing](../../../recipes/adaptive3/review.yaml)

### 3. Independent review earns its cost when it checks what could go wrong

Of 446 assessor task occurrences in build phases, including nested advice, 344 returned passed, 97 changes, two covered, one stuck, and two had no identifiable status in the preview. The change requests spanned 34 builds. They are a mix. A Cortex reviewer found that a drawer change broke navigation elsewhere. A c2j reviewer ran a focused check and reported that a background child survived process rescheduling even after managed verification passed. Another reviewer *removed* a generated requirement for an inspector to prove the freshness of someone else’s inventory. Five builds took assessor corrections and succeeded without Backstop. [Drawer regression](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUSQihJHKW0v6Q6SStjOdUrHMM) · [Process cleanup](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUFFEL5WjDHMIWBFUgHG99mqD1) · [Inventory requirement](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MUFDucdzjLUUqY2dGMBawjtSys)

The drawer reviewer subsequently requested a title change and a correction to the verification sentence, explicitly saying the code passed and no test rerun was needed. The workflow repeated candidate checks anyway. The session count alone cannot explain that expense. Adaptive3’s standard path has a planning author, an implementer and an independent assessor. In the recorded paths there were 607 primary work invocations, 377 main assessment invocations, 149 consultation invocations and 106 Backstop agent invocations. Those are starts and resumptions, not counts of distinct people or models. There was no controlled comparison of two versus three sessions. Keep an independent check on consequential claims. Test fewer mandatory occasions to invoke it. [Session design](../../../recipes/adaptive3/README.md) · [Validation and review routing](../../../recipes/adaptive3/cycle.yaml)

### 4. Formal language makes the important question harder to find

The open question on a blocked Cortex merge was **676 words** and offered two actions, question and revise. The actionable fact was that the original receiver could not use the recovery receipt. Three other open Backstop overviews began under the same “Backstop diagnosis and options” heading and ran to 448, 1,099 and 1,341 words. The Docker one began by saying requester and rationale were unestablished, immediately before reproducing the original request. The reader first had to understand the workflow’s attempt to establish provenance. A healthy Outcome brief in the same six-form snapshot led directly with what accepting the candidate would do. [Open forms and overview readings](inventory.md#what-the-six-open-input-forms-asked-for)

The source asks for accessible prose *and* for document descriptions, per-cell accounting, reconciliation to a frozen plan, negotiation material and recovery sections. The seven Adaptive3 prompt YAML files contain 5,820 whitespace-separated words at the inspected checkout. That is source size across several roles, not tokens sent on one turn. Fresh sessions also receive a shared context block with an extended publication-question procedure as well as a link to its guide. These are concrete places to shorten instructions. Neither the source count nor this observational cohort isolates the effect of the phrase “acceptance criteria.” [Shared context](../../../recipes/adaptive3/prompts/context.yaml) · [Assessor prompt](../../../recipes/adaptive3/prompts/validate.yaml) · [Backstop prompt](../../../recipes/adaptive3/prompts/backstop.yaml)

### 5. Agents knew they needed Docker but chose the wrong way to get it

A root coordination job reached an assessed plan for a disposable OpenTofu environment. A reviewer pointed to the verify hook’s base and capability request. A separate daemon question received essentially the same reply. Another root job requested dedicated hypervisor fixtures and was redirected to the supported ARM/KVM scope. In contrast, several cohort jobs succeeded with an explicit Ubuntu base and Docker capability, and a parser diagnostic succeeded with ARM/KVM. The useful distinction is between a tool missing here, a different worker that can do the task, and an operation that actually needs an operator. A worker allocation also does not grant publication authority. [Docker coordination](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/WRSvGmfDlUhBhZ2lJCOb52AzuD1) · [Daemon reply](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MRjQ3bLfa9ppfSSdo14sKTXgxG) · [Successful Docker check](http://db.lab.c2.run:8081/c2/jobs/3MRw7IfjU0dx5hP2RI42JUvFqU5)

### 6. The tests show how many boundaries a small change can cross

Adaptive3’s native test YAML grew from **6,194 lines in 10 suites** at its November 13 introduction to **19,804 in 42 suites** at consolidation and **30,689 in 79 suites** at this checkout. These counts cover tracked `recipe-tests/adaptive3/*.test.yaml` recursively, excluding fixtures and other test trees. The public build suite alone is 2,075 lines. The retained full-suite report timed the three renewal cases at approximately 43, 72 and 81 seconds each. These are individual case times on that run, not CI wall time. Several near-identical Backstop action cases each boot a runtime to distinguish missing, null, empty and whitespace inputs. Other tests genuinely need native Git, human receipts or child waits. A test-only include substitute is worth piloting at the former boundary. It cannot establish that real publication or recovery works.
